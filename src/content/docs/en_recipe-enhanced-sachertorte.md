## Instructions

Preheat the oven to 170 °C.

1. **Bowl 1 (main mixture):** melt the butter over boiling water.
2. **Bowl 2** (can be a mug as well, but it's slower than melting in a metal bowl): melt the chocolate over boiling water (only for the Sachertorte). You can also add a bit of cocoa for a better, more bitter taste.
3. **Bowl 1:**
   - Add icing sugar and vanilla sugar.
   - Use the mixer while adding the egg yolks.
   - After mixing, add the melted chocolate from bowl 2 and mix with a spatula.
4. **Bowl 3 (egg whites):**
   - The egg whites go here.
   - Use the mixer while adding crystal sugar.
   - After the mixture solidifies a bit and there is no more clear liquid, add it to bowl 1 and fold in with a spatula.
5. **Bowl 1:** sift flour into the mixture and mix with a spatula.
6. **Bowl 4 (for baking):**
   - Butter it out, flour it out.
   - Transfer the content of bowl 1 into it.
   - Bake for 40 minutes.
7. Cut the baked Sachertorte in half (or into thirds) and add apricot jam (and Turkish honey if you have some, it's nice).
8. Make a makeshift holder for the Sachertorte so that the glaze won't stick to the bottom (see the drawing).

![Drawing of the makeshift holder](/img/montaigne/recipes/enhanced-sachertorte/2.jpg)

9. **Bowl 2 (chocolate):** add the rest of the chocolate here with a bit of cocoa if you have any, and melt it over boiling water.
10. **Boiling water pan for the chocolate:**
    - Add sugar to the amount of boiling water specified in the glaze part of the spreadsheet and wait for it to melt.
    - Add the melted chocolate from bowl 2.
    - Mix while boiling until the chocolate starts to stick to the walls of the pan.
    - Quickly transfer onto the Sachertorte.
    - Dip your spatula into the dripped-off chocolate and use it to cover any uncovered parts of the Sachertorte.
11. Let the Sachertorte cool: transfer it to a clean plate and put it in the fridge for at least an hour.
12. Serve.
